Export documents: a beginner's checklist

Export documentation helps identify the goods, record the transaction, support customs procedures and move cargo through the transport chain. The exact document set differs by shipment.

This article is educational. Requirements and commercial specifications vary by destination, buyer and product. Confirm current details before making a shipment or purchase.

Commercial invoice

The commercial invoice records the seller, buyer, product description, quantity, unit price, total value, currency and agreed delivery terms. It should match the contract and other shipment records.

Packing list

The packing list explains how the cargo is packed: number and type of packages, marks, net and gross weights, and dimensions where relevant. It helps logistics providers and customs identify the shipment.

Certificate of origin

A certificate of origin identifies the country of origin under the applicable rules. It may be needed for customs, trade preference claims or buyer requirements. Rules of origin are technical and should be checked for the specific trade arrangement.

Transport document

Depending on the mode of transport, this may be a bill of lading, air waybill, road consignment note or another transport document. It records key details of the carriage and receipt of cargo.

Product-specific certificates

Agricultural and food products may require phytosanitary certificates, inspection reports, fumigation records, health certificates or other documents. Requirements depend on the product and destination country's rules.

Before dispatch

  • Confirm current requirements with the exporting authority and destination importer.
  • Check names, quantities, weights and marks across all documents.
  • Confirm HS classification with a qualified customs source.
  • Keep copies of permits, declarations and transport records.
  • Ask the buyer and freight forwarder to review the document set early.
