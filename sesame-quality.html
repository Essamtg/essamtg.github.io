Sesame quality: what buyers and exporters should understand

Sesame is traded across food, oilseed and ingredient supply chains. Quality is assessed through several factors, and buyer specifications should always be agreed in writing before a deal is finalized.

This article is educational. Requirements and commercial specifications vary by destination, buyer and product. Confirm current details before making a shipment or purchase.

1. Purity and foreign matter

Buyers commonly examine the presence of stones, dust, stems, other seeds and other foreign material. Cleaning and sorting methods should match the buyer’s specification and intended use.

2. Moisture and storage

Moisture affects storage stability and the risk of deterioration. The acceptable limit is not universal: it depends on buyer requirements, local standards, packaging and shipping conditions. Use a calibrated moisture meter and agree on the sampling method.

3. Colour, variety and origin

White, natural, mixed and other commercial descriptions may be used in the market. Clarify the exact appearance, variety or type, origin declaration and any required test results instead of relying on a vague product name.

4. Packaging and traceability

Common export packaging includes woven polypropylene bags or other buyer-approved food-grade packaging. Confirm bag weight, liner requirements, labels, lot identification and container loading practices.

5. Agree on inspection

Specify who samples and inspects the goods, where inspection takes place, what standards apply, and how claims are resolved. A pre-shipment inspection can reduce misunderstandings, but it does not replace a clear contract.

Questions to ask a buyer

  • What exact grade and purity level do you require?
  • What moisture limit and testing method apply?
  • What packaging and bag weight do you accept?
  • Are laboratory tests, certificates or audits required?
  • What are the delivery terms and destination port?
