COFFEE & TEA · EDUCATIONAL GUIDE

A practical guide to exporting coffee from East Africa

Coffee export is more than buying beans and arranging transport. A successful shipment depends on consistent quality, clear contracts, compliant documentation and careful logistics planning.

This article is educational. Requirements and commercial specifications vary by destination, buyer and product. Confirm current details before making a shipment or purchase.

1. Understand the coffee you are trading

Start by identifying the species and commercial grade. East Africa produces both Arabica and Robusta. Buyers may specify screen size, defects, moisture, cup profile, processing method, crop year and packaging. Never assume that a broad label such as “Robusta” is a complete specification.

2. Agree on quality before purchase

Request a written specification and representative sample. Agree how quality will be assessed, who performs inspection, how disputes are handled and what happens if the delivered lot falls outside the agreed limits. Keep a retained sample from the approved lot where practical.

3. Prepare the commercial documents

Common documents may include a sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, phytosanitary or other regulatory certificates where applicable, and transport documents. The exact requirements depend on the product, origin, destination and current rules.

4. Plan the route and costs

Calculate inland transport, handling, warehousing, inspection, customs procedures, freight, insurance and destination charges. Ask the freight provider for a written quotation that identifies what is included and excluded. Compare routes on total landed cost, reliability and transit time—not freight price alone.

5. Protect the transaction

Check the buyer and seller, agree payment terms, define delivery terms using the appropriate Incoterms rule, and ensure that responsibility for risk, costs and documents is understood by both parties.

Pre-shipment checklist

  • Written product specification and approved sample
  • Confirmed quantity, packaging and shipment window
  • Signed contract and agreed payment terms
  • Verified export and import requirements
  • Freight quotation and cargo insurance decision
  • Document review before dispatch
